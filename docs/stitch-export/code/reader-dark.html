The ship drifted in the vast, velvet silence of the Galactic Core. Golan Trevize watched the viewscreen with a mixture of awe and trepidation. The stars here were not the lonely sparks of the Perimeter; they were a dense, brilliant tapestry of fire, a cosmic ocean of light that seemed to pulse with its own rhythm.

"Intelligence is not just the ability to process information, but the wisdom to know which information to ignore."

Pelorat sat beside him, his fingers dancing across the terminal, his face illuminated by the soft blue glow of the AI's interface. "It's remarkable, Golan. The data patterns here suggest a civilization that didn't just inhabit space, they curated it. Look at these gravitational harmonics. This isn't natural."

Trevize didn't answer immediately. He was thinking of Gaia, of the collective consciousness that now hummed at the edge of his own thoughts. The choice he had made back on Gaia felt heavier here, in the cold reality of physical space. Was a unified mind the ultimate evolution, or the ultimate cage?

The AI, Bliss, appeared as a soft shimmer in the corner of the cabin. Her voice was like silk, a quiet resonance that seemed to come from everywhere and nowhere. "The Curator's path is never linear, Trevize. You seek a destination, but you are creating the map as you go."

"And what if I don't like the map?" Trevize asked, his eyes never leaving the stars.

"Then you fold the paper and start again," Bliss replied. "But remember, every fold leaves a crease. You cannot return to a perfectly smooth surface once the journey has begun."
